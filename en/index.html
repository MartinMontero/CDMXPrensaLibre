Top priority

⚠️ Security first (OPSEC)

Operational security is not optional or an appendix — it is the foundation. Read this whole section before touching any sensitive document. Recommendations draw on recognized guides (EFF, Freedom of the Press Foundation, Access Now, CPJ).

1 · Model your threat before acting

You cannot protect everything against everyone. EFF Surveillance Self-Defense proposes five questions for your security plan:

  1. What do I want to protect? (assets: documents, source identities, location).
  2. From whom? (your adversary: an authority, a developer, organized crime, an official).
  3. How likely is it that I will need to protect it?
  4. How bad are the consequences if I fail?
  5. How much effort am I willing to spend to prevent them?

In Mexico the adversary can be sophisticated and state-linked: Pegasus spyware infections of journalists, lawyers and defenders have been documented. The phone is both an asset and an attack surface — network anonymity is moot if the device is compromised.

🔴 The most underestimated risk: pasting documents into a consumer AI

Consumer AI tools retain and may train on what you type. In 2025 a U.S. court ordered OpenAI to preserve even deleted consumer chats (NYT v. OpenAI); Enterprise/Edu accounts and zero-retention API were exempt. Anthropic, since September 2025, trains on consumer conversations by default unless you opt out (retention up to 5 years if accepted; 30 days if declined); its commercial/API tiers are excluded. Consumer Google Gemini also trains by default.

Harm reduction (do this):

  • Anonymize before uploading. Replace victims' names, addresses, CURP, RFC and phone numbers with labels ([VICTIM-01], [PROPERTY-A]) and keep the key only on your encrypted device.
  • Do not upload raw source PDFs containing victim data to consumer chatbots. Process first (extract only the text you need).
  • Prefer local models (Ollama, GPT4All) that send nothing to the cloud, or enterprise/API tiers with zero retention.
  • Turn off history and the training option in the tool's settings.
  • Use an account not tied to your real identity when possible.

Specialist guidance: Freedom of the Press — “Using AI safely as a journalist”.

2 · Browse and access without leaving a trace

🧅 Tor Browser

Hides your IP and location. Download only from torproject.org. Only what you do inside Tor Browser is anonymous; do not log into identifying accounts or add extensions.

💽 Tails

An “amnesic” operating system that boots from USB, routes everything through Tor and leaves no trace on shutdown. tails.net (part of the Tor Project since 2024). Includes a metadata cleaner.

🛡️ A VPN is not anonymity

A VPN shifts who you trust (your ISP for the VPN company) but does not make you anonymous. For anonymity, use Tor. If you use a VPN, pick one with a verifiable no-logs policy.

3 · Communicate with encryption

  • Signal (signal.org): end-to-end encrypted by default with minimal metadata. Enable disappearing messages, verify the safety number with your contacts (ideally in person), set a PIN and registration lock, and use usernames to avoid sharing your phone number.
  • Avoid SMS and regular calls: they are unencrypted and interceptable by the carrier (SS7 weaknesses) or via SIM swap. For two-factor authentication, prefer an authenticator app or a hardware key, never SMS.
  • Encrypted email (PGP): does not hide the subject, sender or recipient. For the most sensitive matters, use Signal instead of email.

5 · Accounts and passwords

  • Use a password manager (KeePassXC, Bitwarden) with a long passphrase (6+ random words) and unique passwords per site.
  • Enable two-factor authentication; prefer hardware keys (FIDO2/passkeys) or an authenticator app over SMS.
  • Beware of phishing: check the real domain of every link; watch for near-identical lookalike domains.

6 · Protect sources and victims

  • Minimize the personal data you collect and keep. Obtain informed consent from people at risk, and never visually “black-box” redact — flatten/rasterize the document.
  • For anonymous submissions there are SecureDrop (over Tor) and, in Mexico, Méxicoleaks (a media/civil-society coalition on GlobaLeaks/Tor). Verify any leak platform is live and monitored before referring a source to it.

The guide you produce can identify active corruption actors and mechanisms. Section 1 (actors) and Section 6B (cover-up) are especially sensitive. Overview

How it works

Three moments: prepare your documents, load them in phases into the AI tool, and run the synthesis prompt to get a structured guide in 9 sections (0 to 8).

  1. Prepare and clean your source documents, give each an ID (Doc-01, Doc-02…) and anonymize them if they contain victim data.
  2. Load in phases: first ingest each document one at a time (without analyzing), then paste the full prompt and run the synthesis.
  3. Verify and use: review each section with the checklist, validate the legal framework with a lawyer, and take the field card to meetings.
⚠️ Guiding principle

The prompt does not invent or speculate: it only extracts, organizes and verifies what is in your documents. Output quality depends directly on input quality. Undated, unattributed or second-hand documents contaminate the result and weaken your credibility.

How to prepare each document

  1. Assign an ID to each document: Doc-01, Doc-02… It is your citation key throughout the guide.
  2. Build a control sheet with: ID, full title, issuing organization, exact date, geographic scope (borough/neighborhood) and evidentiary type.
  3. Confirm PDFs are selectable (not scanned without OCR). If scanned, run OCR before uploading.
  4. Number the pages if they lack numbering: the prompt cites by page.
  5. Note key tables, charts or images by hand: the model may not read them well as images.
  6. Anonymize victims' sensitive data before uploading (see Security).
🚫 What NOT to include

Do not upload documents without a date, identifiable author or verifiable source, or clearly second-hand material without citing its origin. They will contaminate the analysis and may compromise the guide's credibility.

Step 5

Legal verification

Laws and institution names change. The model can be wrong or out of date. Always verify the critical points independently.

📌 Without web access, every article is “pending”

Many AI tools cannot browse, and several official portals (.gob.mx) block automated access. If the model could not verify, it must flag 📌 [pending verification]. Your legal team must confirm all those articles in the official source before using the guide.

  • Property title: check the RPPC-CDMX. Real folios change.
  • Sanctions on public servants: DeclaraNet may be outdated; call the agency's internal control body.
  • Status of amparo or nullity suits: check the judiciary portal or the TFJA/TJACDMX with the case number.
  • Official directory: roles change; verify on the agency's official page.

The full directory of verified official sources is in Legal framework & sources.

Three common red flags

⚠️ Inference

Phrases like “likely,” “apparently,” “it can be inferred.” Order: “Remove all inference; facts with citations only.”

⚠️ Citing from memory

An article with no URL or 📌 flag: the model may be citing outdated text.

⚠️ Invented Section 7

Gaps not present in any source: the model is generating its own analysis, which is prohibited.

Legal framework & official sources

A verified directory of institutions, legislative sources and the rights framework. Always confirm the current text in the official source before using it in a case: laws are reformed and institutions are renamed.

📅 Recent institutional changes (do not use abolished names)
  • INAI was abolished (constitutional reform, DOF 20/11/2024). The federal access-to-information authority is now Transparencia para el Pueblo, part of the Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno (SABG).
  • The Secretaría de la Función Pública (SFP) is now the SABG (DOF 28/11/2024). DeclaraNet keeps operating.
  • INFO CDMX was abolished by the CDMX Congress (Dec 2025) and replaced by the Instituto de Transparencia para el Pueblo de la Ciudad de México, now under the city Comptroller. The transition was still underway as of June 2026; requests still go through the PNT.
  • There is no court called “TECA”. The federal administrative court is the TFJA; the local one is the TJACDMX.

For journalists and activists

Red-flags glossary

Terms to use precisely when reporting. These definitions are indicative; in a specific case, confirm the legal meaning with the applicable law and a lawyer.

TermMeaning
Acto de molestiaAn act of authority that temporarily affects rights; requires a written, founded and reasoned order (CPEUM Art. 16).
Acto privativoAn act that permanently deprives a right; requires prior trial with due process (CPEUM Art. 14).
DespojoOccupying real property or exercising another's real right through violence, deceit or stealth (CPF 395 / CPDF 237).
Desalojo forzoso (forced eviction)Removing people from their home without adequate legal protection; prima facie incompatible with the ICESCR (CESCR General Comment No. 7).
Fraude procesalSimulating acts or altering evidence to mislead a judicial or administrative authority (CPDF 310).
Cohecho (bribery)A public servant requesting/receiving an undue benefit to act or omit (CPF 222).
Peculado (embezzlement)A public servant diverting or appropriating public resources in their charge (CPF 223).
Abuso de autoridadA public servant unlawfully exercising powers or using violence without legitimate cause (CPF 215).
Amparo indirectoAmparo before a District Court against acts of authority that are not final judgments; admits suspension.
Suspensión (provisional/definitive)Measure that halts the challenged act while the amparo is decided; the provisional one is immediate.

Version control

  1. When adding new documents, re-run Section 0 first, with all of them included.
  2. Check whether new documents contradict or reinforce Sections 1, 2 or 3.
  3. Update the field card date (§8).
  4. Notify users of the change and archive the prior version with a date for traceability.
